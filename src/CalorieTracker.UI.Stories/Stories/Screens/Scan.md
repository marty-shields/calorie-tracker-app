# Scan

Scans a product's barcode and, when the product is found, goes straight to the Serving screen (no values screen in between). View: `ScanView`; page: `ScanPage`.

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Top bar | The title "Scan barcode", a back arrow (and Android back) to where Scan was opened from (an in-app `back` route, else Today on that day), no navigation bar, and the centred chip "Adding to <meal> · <day>". |
| Content | **Opening camera**: a spinner labelled "Opening camera…" until the native camera covers the screen. **Closing camera**: a spinner labelled "Closing camera…" while the camera slides away after Cancel or Android back, so the screen never says "Opening camera…" on the way out. **Looking up**: a spinner; a found product keeps it up while the route changes to Serving, so no product is ever shown here. **Not found**: an `EmptyState` with "Unable to find this product" and the hint "Try another barcode, or search by name.", with no reason, then a primary **Search by name instead** (`scan-search`; it goes back to the search that opened Scan, typed text included, and otherwise opens a new search for the meal and day, see below) above a secondary **Scan again** (`scan-again`). A product found but not saved (no food id) shows this too. **Camera unavailable**: `LoadFailed` "Couldn't open the camera" with **Try again**, which restarts the scan (permission refused, or the scanner could not open), and **Search by name instead** under it. |
| Native camera | Over the Content while the camera is open: a centred "Adding to <meal> · <day>" pill at the top (`ScanCaption`: white with dark text in both themes, from the `PillBackground` and `PillText` colours in `App.xaml`), the frame, the hint "Point the camera at a barcode", and in the bottom bar an outlined **Search by name instead** (`scan-native-search`; same target as the web button) above **Cancel** (`scan-native-cancel`). |
| Found | The page asks for the food's last amount in the route's meal and goes to `/serving/<id>?meal=&date=&back=<Today on that day>[&amount=&servings=&serving=]`. Serving shows the food's values and its favourite star. Back from Serving goes to Today on that day (not back into the camera, which would lose the product), and so does a save. |

**One food for many barcodes.** A drink is sold in several sizes, each with its own barcode. A looked-up product is stored by `FoodImportService` (Core), which the scan only calls; it knows nothing about the camera. A barcode already stored shows its food, and the product is not stored again. For a new barcode the service looks for a stored Open Food Facts food that is the same product: the same unit, the same first brand after folding (not blank), the same name after folding with size words such as "330 ml", "1.5L" and "2 litres" taken out, and kcal, protein, carbs and fat, and fibre, sugars, saturates and salt, each within 2% of the larger value or 0.1, whichever is more (a nutrient known on one side only does not match). If there is one, the new barcode is added to that food with its pack; otherwise a new food is stored as before. Zero, Light, diet, sugar-free, flavour and "original taste" variants differ in name or nutrients, so they never merge, and a food is never merged across g and ml. The stored food keeps its name, brand and nutrients, and a later refresh of one barcode never writes another barcode's values to it. Each barcode keeps its pack (amount and label) on its own row, and no serving is stored for it. A found product opens the Serving screen on the serving last used for that food, with its last amount and servings, when it was logged before by servings and still has that serving, else on the person's smallest own serving, else in grams or ml (the scanned pack is not preselected). A product with no name, unit or needed nutrient is not stored, and the scan shows not found for it as it did before. Foods already stored as separate rows are not merged afterwards, and there is no screen to undo a merge.

The camera stays open until it sees a barcode of a valid length (8, 12 or 13 digits); other codes are ignored. That one barcode is normalised and looked up, and later detections are ignored: the scanner closes on the first accepted code, so no separate in-flight guard is needed. Not found, offline and rate limited all show the one not-found message, with no mention of the source or the reason. Cancelling the camera goes back as the back arrow does. The native camera screen mirrors the web view: a centred "Adding to Dinner · 18 Nov" pill at the top (the page passes the same text to the scanner, `ScanCaption`), the frame, the hint "Point the camera at a barcode", and in the bottom bar an outlined **Search by name instead** (`scan-native-search`) above **Cancel** (`scan-native-cancel`). Search by name instead closes the camera with the `SearchByName` status and the page goes where the web button goes (`ScanRoute.SearchTarget`): back to the search that opened Scan, typed text included, when Scan was opened from search, else `/foods?meal=&date=`; Cancel and Android back go back. The web chip is centred too. The native camera screen follows the app's chosen theme (System follows the phone): the page passes the saved choice to the scanner, which sets it as the app theme before opening the camera.
